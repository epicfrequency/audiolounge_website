General

What is Audio Lounge?

An elegant music player for people who own and care about their music. Audio Lounge 1.1 is available on Mac; Audio Lounge Air for iPad is coming soon.

Is Audio Lounge Air a Mac remote?

No. It is the iPadOS edition of Audio Lounge, with its own library and playback experience.

Is there a subscription?

No. Start free; a one-time purchase unlocks unlimited playback.

Library organization

How are album and artist counts calculated?

Counts reflect Audio Lounge’s organized library view rather than a simple raw-file count. Tracks that belong to one grouped release are counted as that release, so the number can differ from the number of folders or files on disk.

How are multi-disc releases handled?

Discs that belong to the same release are grouped into one album when the available metadata identifies them as one release.

What happens to loose tracks in one folder?

Loose tracks that belong together can be grouped as one album/library item rather than creating unnecessary separate entries.

Does Audio Lounge modify my music files?

No. Audio Lounge builds its library view around the locations you choose and leaves the original music files in place.

SMB Direct

Does SMB Direct require Finder or Files app mounting?

No. Audio Lounge connects directly to the SMB share.

What is the difference between Share and Folder inside share?

The Share is the SMB resource exported by the NAS, such as Music. Folder inside share is an optional subfolder within it, such as Classical.

What should I enter for Domain?

Most home NAS users should leave Domain blank. Enter it only when the SMB server or managed network specifically requires one.

What happens if the NAS is temporarily offline?

The library location becomes unavailable until the share is reachable again; Audio Lounge does not move the source library to its own cloud service.

HALO Network Audio

Does HALO require a server or core?

No. Audio Lounge remains the player. HALO Endpoint is a lightweight output endpoint.

Which endpoint platforms are supported?

64-bit Linux on ARM64 and x86-64. DietPi, Raspberry Pi OS 64-bit and Ubuntu are the recommended platforms.

What stays in Audio Lounge?

The library, queue, decoding, optional PCM upsampling and DoP preparation stay on the Audio Lounge side. HALO extends the resulting audio path to the DAC.

Does HALO change the Mac’s normal system output?

No. HALO is selected inside Audio Lounge, so the normal system output can remain assigned to another device.

Technical

Which formats are supported?

FLAC, ALAC, WAV, AIFF, APE, WavPack, AAC, MP3, CUE, DSF, DFF and SACD ISO.

What does bit-perfect mean in Audio Lounge?

On supported hardware and output configurations, Audio Lounge can send the decoded audio without sample-rate conversion or other processing. Enabling optional processing such as PCM upsampling intentionally changes that path.

Does Audio Lounge switch sample rate automatically?

Yes, on supported output paths and devices, Audio Lounge can match the hardware sample rate to the source.

How are DSD and DoP handled?

Audio Lounge supports DSF/DFF and SACD ISO. Compatible USB DACs can receive DSD using DoP; DSD-to-PCM conversion is also available where appropriate.

Does Audio Lounge support PCM upsampling?

Yes. PCM upsampling is optional; when enabled, the Transparent Audio Path shows the resulting output format and sample rate.

Does Audio Lounge support gapless playback?

Yes, for compatible tracks and formats.

What is Transparent Audio Path?

It shows the source format, playback/processing path, selected output and final sample rate in real time, including HALO when used.
