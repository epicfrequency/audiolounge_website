Remote Mode in Audio Lounge 1.1.7

Browse the music on your Mac and control playback from your iPhone or iPad.

1. Enable Remote Control on your Mac

Open Audio Lounge → Settings → Remote Setup and turn on Allow iPhone and iPad Remote Control. Leave Audio Lounge open and keep your Mac awake. You can confirm the connection under Connected controllers on this page.

Allow Remote Audio Setup is optional. Leave it off if you only want to browse and control playback. Enable it if you also want to change the Mac’s audio settings from Air.

2. Connect from your iPhone or iPad

  1. Use Audio Lounge 1.1.7 or later on your Mac and Audio Lounge Air 1.1.7 or later on your iPhone or iPad. Join the same local network on both devices.
  2. On iPhone, tap Remote at the right end of the bottom tab bar. On iPad, tap Remote at the right end of the top tab bar. Then tap Enter Remote Mode. Allow Local Network access if prompted so Air can find your Mac.
  3. Air can connect automatically to a nearby player. If you need to choose a player, open the connection menu at the top left and select your Mac from the available players.

Choose your Mac

If Air did not connect to the Mac you want, tap the connection icon at the top left and choose your Mac from the player list. Once connected, Library shows that Mac’s albums.

If your Mac does not appear, check that Remote Control is enabled, Audio Lounge is still open, and Air has Local Network access. Keeping the apps on the same version is recommended.

3. Find an album and build a queue

Open Library in Remote Mode to browse the Mac’s collection. Switch between album and folder browsing, search for an album, or narrow the view with filters. Open an album to play it or add it to the queue. Queue shows what the Mac will play next.

4. Control playback from across the room

Now Playing shows what the Mac is playing and lets you pause, skip, or seek. If the Mac is connected to a larger display, choose Show Player Full Screen in Now Playing to put Now Playing on that display. Choose Restore Player Window to exit full screen on the Mac.

Why Remote Mode feels familiar

The Mac organizes albums and folders, maintains the queue, and handles playback and audio output. Air displays the Mac’s library results, artwork, and playback state using layouts that fit the iPhone or iPad. The phone does not need its own copy of the music library or a separate scan.

This makes it practical to leave the Mac by your audio system. Pick a record, line up the next album, and check what’s playing from the couch. If the Mac is using a DAC or HALO, it continues to use that output while Air acts as the controller.

To return to music stored on your iPhone or iPad, open the connection menu and switch back to the local player.
