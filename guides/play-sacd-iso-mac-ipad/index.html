How to Play SACD ISO Files on Mac and iPad

An SACD collection does not have to be split into thousands of DSF files before it becomes usable. With a player that understands the disc image, the original ISO can remain intact on local storage, an external drive or a NAS.

What is an SACD ISO?

An SACD ISO is a disc image: a sector-by-sector representation of a Super Audio CD. Instead of storing each song as an independent file, the image can retain the disc structure, track boundaries and stereo or multichannel areas that were present on the original release.

The audio inside an SACD is DSD rather than conventional PCM. SACD commonly uses DSD64, whose 1-bit stream runs at 2.8224 MHz. Some discs store that stream directly; others use DST, a lossless compression method designed for DSD. DST reduces storage requirements without turning DSD into PCM, but a player needs an appropriate decoder to read it.

Why ordinary players may not open it

An ISO is a container for an entire disc, not a normal single-track audio file. A compatible player must understand the SACD filesystem, read its table of contents, choose the stereo or multichannel program, identify tracks and decode the DSD payload. A player that supports FLAC or even standalone DSF files does not automatically support SACD ISO.

SACD ISO versus extracting to DSF

Extraction is useful, but it is not inherently required. Direct ISO playback keeps the disc in one archival object and avoids creating a large folder of per-track files. It can also preserve the original disc organization while making backup and library management simpler.

DSF may still be the better choice when you want one file per track, broad compatibility with tag editors, portable-player support, or detailed metadata and artwork embedded in each song. Conversion is therefore a workflow choice, not a prerequisite for hearing the album.

Keep the master, choose the workflow.

Many collectors retain the original ISO as the archival copy and extract DSF only for devices or software that require individual files.

How to play SACD ISO on Mac

  1. Keep the ISO on the Mac, an attached external drive, or a configured NAS share.
  2. Add that location to Audio Lounge and scan it into the library.
  3. Open the resulting album and select a track; Audio Lounge reads supported SACD ISO images directly rather than requiring a DSF extraction step.
  4. Choose the appropriate local output or a HALO Endpoint.
  5. For a USB DAC, select a DSD mode the hardware accepts. Depending on the device and path, that may be Native DSD, DoP or DSD-to-PCM.

Gapless playback is available for compatible tracks and formats. Whether a particular SACD image transitions seamlessly also depends on its track boundaries and the selected output path.

For the underlying formats and output modes, read the complete guide to DSD playback on Mac.

How to play SACD ISO directly from a NAS

SACD ISO files can be several gigabytes, so a NAS is often a more practical home than the Mac’s internal storage. The NAS only needs to expose the music folder through SMB; it does not need a proprietary music-server application just for Audio Lounge.

With SMB Direct, Audio Lounge connects to the share itself. On Mac, you do not have to mount that share manually in Finder first. Add the host, share and optional subfolder in Audio Lounge, scan the location, and browse the result as part of the same library as local music.

How to play SACD ISO on iPad

Audio Lounge Air is the iPad edition of the player, not a remote for a Mac. Its SMB Direct workflow lets the iPad connect to the NAS library itself, so a large SACD collection can remain on network storage instead of being copied into the iPad’s limited local space.

  1. Make sure the iPad and NAS are reachable on the same local network.
  2. Add the SMB share inside Audio Lounge Air; no prior Files app mount is required.
  3. Scan the location and open the supported SACD ISO from the organized library.
  4. Select a compatible output path. For a Hi-Fi system elsewhere in the room, HALO can send the prepared stream to an Endpoint beside the DAC.

Network playback with HALO

HALO is Audio Lounge’s high-resolution network audio system. Audio Lounge on the Mac or iPad remains responsible for the library, queue, decoding and output decision. A lightweight Linux Endpoint beside the USB DAC receives the prepared stream.

NAS→Audio Lounge on Mac / iPad→HALO→USB DAC

HALO supports bit-perfect PCM, DoP and, with compatible endpoint hardware and DACs, Native DSD up to DSD512. The maximum useful format is determined by the source, Endpoint and DAC together. See the HALO setup guide and compatibility details.

SACD ISO FAQ

Do I need to convert SACD ISO to DSF?

No. A player with direct SACD ISO support can read the image and present its albums and tracks without extracting them to DSF first. DSF remains useful for per-track files and broader tagging compatibility.

Can Apple Music play SACD ISO?

No. Apple Music does not treat an SACD ISO disc image as a supported music-library format. Use a player with explicit SACD ISO support.

Can I play SACD ISO directly from NAS?

Yes. Audio Lounge can read a supported SACD ISO from an SMB share on Mac or iPad, so the image can remain on the NAS.

Does Audio Lounge support DST-compressed SACD ISO?

Audio Lounge publicly documents SACD ISO support, but its current public specifications do not separately guarantee every DST-compressed image. Test a representative album from your library before planning a large migration.

Can SACD ISO playback be gapless?

Audio Lounge supports gapless playback for compatible tracks and formats. The result still depends on the image, track boundaries and selected output path.
