NAS · SMB · Mac & iPad

How to Play Music from a NAS on Mac and iPad

A NAS can hold one music library for the whole home without turning that library into a streaming service. The simplest path is often ordinary file sharing: the player reads the files from an SMB share and keeps playback decisions on the Mac or iPad.

NAS versus local storage

A local library lives on the Mac, iPad or an attached drive. It is straightforward and remains available without the network, but every device needs access to its own copy or to the attached disk.

A network-attached storage device keeps the files in one place and makes them available over the local network. This is useful for large lossless collections, shared household access and computers with limited internal storage. The tradeoff is that the NAS and network must be available when you listen.

SMB in simple terms

SMB is a standard network file-sharing protocol. A NAS publishes a named share—often something simple such as Music—and a client connects with a hostname or IP address, username and password.

On a traditional Mac workflow, you connect to the server in Finder, mount the share as a volume, and then point the music application at that mounted path. It works, but the mount can disappear after logout, sleep or a network change, and an iPad has a different Files-based workflow.

What Audio Lounge SMB Direct changes

SMB Direct stores the NAS connection inside Audio Lounge. The app connects to the share itself rather than depending on a Finder or Files mount created beforehand.

  • On Mac, there is no requirement to mount the share manually in Finder first.
  • On iPad, there is no requirement to mount it in the Files app first.
  • The NAS does not need a proprietary music-server package just for Audio Lounge.
  • Local, external-drive and NAS locations can appear within the same organized library.

The NAS still needs ordinary SMB file sharing enabled, and the account must have permission to read the music folder. See the detailed SMB Direct setup guide for host, share, folder and credential examples.

Why direct NAS access helps large libraries

FLAC and other lossless PCM formats are larger than typical streaming files, but are usually easy for a modern wired or reliable Wi-Fi network to deliver. The player reads the source, decodes it and supplies the selected audio output.

DSF and DFF streams are larger still. A supported SACD ISO may contain an entire stereo or multichannel disc and occupy several gigabytes. Keeping those images on the NAS avoids duplicating a large archive across every Mac and iPad.

For DSD transport modes, DAC requirements and rate terminology, see how to play DSD on Mac.

Mac workflow

  1. Enable SMB on the NAS and create or identify the share that contains the library.
  2. In Audio Lounge, add an SMB share using the NAS hostname or IP address, share name, optional subfolder and credentials.
  3. Scan the location. Audio Lounge builds its library view while leaving the source files on the NAS.
  4. Browse albums, search, queue music and select a local USB DAC or HALO Endpoint.

If the NAS becomes unavailable, its library location remains offline until the share can be reached again; Audio Lounge does not upload or silently copy the source collection to a cloud service.

iPad workflow

Audio Lounge Air has its own library and playback experience. Add the same SMB share inside the iPad app and scan it over the local network. The collection stays on the NAS, which is especially useful when the library is much larger than the iPad’s storage.

The iPad and NAS must be mutually reachable. Reliable Wi-Fi is usually sufficient for normal lossless playback, while very high-rate DSD and large network workloads benefit from a stable network and sensible buffering.

Local USB DAC or HALO network output

On Mac, a compatible USB DAC can be selected as the local output. Supported paths can use automatic sample-rate matching, bit-perfect PCM, DoP or Native DSD according to the source and hardware.

HALO is optional. It moves the output endpoint beside the Hi-Fi while Audio Lounge remains the player on Mac or iPad:

NAS→Audio Lounge on Mac / iPad→HALO→USB DAC

With compatible endpoint hardware and DACs, HALO supports bit-perfect PCM, DoP and Native DSD up to DSD512. Read the HALO overview and endpoint setup guide before choosing endpoint hardware.
